F-08 · AI-Augmented BA Operating Toolkit

Skill Investment Matrix

Where to spend development time: invest, maintain, build selectively, or divest.

01 · What problem it solves

Once you know where your tasks fall, the next question is which skills to build, maintain, or deliberately stop investing in. The matrix maps competencies against market demand and AI replaceability, and its discipline is symmetrical: it names not only the moat to deepen but the proficiencies to hold at maintenance level and the activities to divest — the decision most development plans avoid.

02 · How it works

Low AI replaceabilityHigh AI replaceability
High market demandINVEST HEAVILY — deepen differentiating capabilityMAINTAIN — stay proficient; avoid excessive depth
Low market demandBUILD SELECTIVELY — tomorrow's differentiatorsDIVEST — minimize time aggressively

Score each skill 1–5 on both axes with evidence — job postings, role expectations, manager feedback, industry signals — then commit a 90-day portfolio: three priority skills with a named practice project and evidence of improvement, one maintain-only skill, and one activity to stop over-investing in.

03 · Worked example

The logistics analyst scores eight competencies. INVEST: freight-domain data expertise, AI governance for the order-automation program, stakeholder influence with carrier partners. MAINTAIN: process mapping, sprint facilitation — proficient today, no further depth. BUILD SELECTIVELY: AI output validation for operational decisions. DIVEST: hand-built status reporting and template maintenance, with the time moved to the governance work. The 90-day portfolio names one deliverable per invest skill — e.g., authoring the autonomy boundaries for the duplicate-shipment automation identified in triage.
